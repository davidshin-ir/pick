01Initial CT finding

Are CT findings concerning for intestinal wall necrosis present (pneumatosis, pneumoperitoneum, or portal venous gas)?

These findings can raise concern for severe bowel ischemia or necrosis but do not independently prove transmural necrosis. The source figure directs patients with intestinal wall necrosis to emergency laparotomy; correlate with clinical findings and urgent surgical assessment.

02Initial CT phenotype

With no intestinal wall necrosis, which CT pattern is present?

Adapted criterion: either ascites or bowel wall edema selects the endovascular pathway. Figure 6 originally specifies both findings without collaterals.

0324-hour CT reassessment

What does the repeat CT at approximately 24 hours show?

The 24-hour CT reassessment is depicted only for the initially uncomplicated, anticoagulation-first branch.

Original figure, interpretation notes, and definitions

Source: Köhler M, Maksoud Z, Nacul Mora G, Pöhler G. Interventional management of acute mesenteric venous thrombosis: a systematic review of current evidence, emerging techniques and future perspectives. Abdominal Radiology. 2026. doi:10.1007/s00261-026-05786-6. Figure 6 presents the institutional AMVT management decision tree. The authors explicitly state that the figure represents local interdisciplinary practice and is not a formal, evidence-graded recommendation.

Assumptions: CT-confirmed AMVT is required before entering the tree. The published figure depicts (1) no ascites, bowel wall edema, or necrosis; (2) both ascites and bowel wall edema without collaterals; and (3) intestinal wall necrosis. This interactive version intentionally broadens branch (2) to ascites OR bowel wall edema at the user’s request; the expanded criterion is not directly validated by the published figure.

Labels: AMVT = acute mesenteric venous thrombosis; CE-CT = contrast-enhanced computed tomography. The source depicts systemic anticoagulation downstream of all three treatment branches, followed by CE-CT at 7 days, 30 days, and 6 months. Whether and when anticoagulation is clinically safe is not specified in the figure.

For educational reference only. This interactive pathway is not a formal medical recommendation and does not replace individualized clinical judgment.
