Practice essentials

Best practice habits

  1. Don’t lose access.
  2. Use small things to achieve big.
  3. Be in line.
  4. No need to be “better” when already “good.”
  5. Don’t force it.
  6. Know where the ends of your devices are.
  7. Beware of the points of no return.
  8. Minimize your radiation dose by minimizing the patient’s.
  9. Pay attention to your patient throughout the procedure.
  10. Keep the back table organized.

Procedure care

Benign biliary stricture protocol

General guideline

  • Initial drain: 10 F internal-external
  • Upsize: Cholangiogram q2w until plasty to ~10 mm and tube to 14-16 F. Cap after 48 hours.
  • Maintenance: Cholangiogram every 2 months with prn touch-up plasty and tube exchange for 6 months at minimum. (The 6-month clock may restart depending on the cholangiogram appearance and need for repeated plasty.)
  • Downsize: If ready, get to 8 F D-M external or 4-5 F Kumpe placeholder over 1-2 months.
  • Removal: Cap for additional 1 month, then out.
Procedure care

Large-volume paracentesis (>5 L)

  • Infuse IV albumin 25%
  • 7 g per liter of ascitic fluid removed, or 50 g, whichever is greater

Discharge prescriptions

Standard (e.g. for post-embo syndrome)

  • Oxycodone 5 mg q6h PRN pain, 20 tabs
  • Ondansetron 4 mg q6h PRN nausea, 20 tabs
  • Senna 8.6 mg 1-2 tabs qhs PRN constipation, 20 tabs

Partial/Distal Splenic Embo

  • + Augmentin x 7d

UFE

  • + Naproxen 500mg BID, 10 tabs
Procedure care

Antithrombotics after venous recanalization

  • If any new stents: ASA 81 for 6 months to life
  • If any significant acute/subacute thrombus burden before or during intervention or suboptimal post-intervention flow: Therapeutic AC for at least 3-6 months.
  • If recurrent DVT, thrombophilia, reintervention: AC for life
  • If post declot of a dialysis access, no routine AP/AC.
  • If post TIPS-PVR in cirrhotic, can avoid AC unless significant thrombus burden during intervention, residual chronic obstruction, or transplant candidate.
Procedure care

Post-procedure follow-up

TIPS (creation, revision, reduction)

  • In the procedure note and POD1 progress note, recommend TIPS duplex ultrasound at 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, then every 6 months. No need for separate IR F/U orders/imaging/visits.

TIPS with more complex variceal interventions (e.g. transvenous obliteration)

  • IR clinic F/U with CT a/p with contrast at 1 month in addition to the above.

PVR-TIPS

  • Routine POD1 CT4P Liver if transsplenic access, challenging procedure, and/or concern for significant residual thrombus.
  • IR clinic F/U with CT4P Liver and TIPS duplex ultrasound at 1 month. Then CT a/p with IV contrast and TIPS duplex 3 months after (+/- clinic visit). If patent, recommend TIPS duplex ultrasound surveillance another 3 months after, then every 6 months (per referring).

BRTO variant (without TIPS)

  • Depending on the technical success of the procedure, may obtain CT a/p with contrast on POD2.
  • IR clinic F/U with CT a/p with contrast at 1 month. Recommend EGD at 1-2 months (for assessment of EV development/aggravation).

Venous

  • Routine POD1 venous duplex ultrasound of the affected limb(s)
  • IR clinic F/U (Dr. Chick) with venous duplex ultrasound of the affected limb(s) and CTV of chest or a/p at 1 month.

Requests & templates

Lower GI bleeding consult note

Brief IR Consult Note

_yo _ with h/o _, presenting with melena/hematochezia. IR was consulted for consideration of angioembolization. The medical record and imaging were reviewed.

A recent CTA demonstrates no arterial extravasation into the GI tract. The patient is hemodynamically stable. For lower GI bleeding, empiric or non-selective embolization should be avoided due to the risk of bowel ischemia/infarct.

Recommendations:

Continue to monitor the vitals and H/H.

Maintain platelet > 50 and INR < 1.8. Discontinue/reverse any anticoagulation.

If the patient experiences hemodynamic instability and/or massive clinical bleeding, then emergent angioembolization can be considered. No repeat CTA is necessary.

Abscess drainage consult note

Brief IR Consult Note

_yo _ with h/o _, presenting with a fluid collection in _, suspicious for abscess. IR was consulted for consideration of percutaneous drain placement. The medical record and imaging were reviewed.

The collection of interest is not amenable to percutaneous drainage given its location/size.

Recommendations:

Continue medical management with IV antibiotics.

If there is no clinical improvement, consider repeat imaging and reconsult IR for review.

Documentation

Dictation tips

  • Simplify drain French sizes to the nearest whole numbers (e.g. 8, 10, 12 F).
  • Leave the presence statement empty; attendings will complete this as appropriate.
  • Specify if contrast use was nonvascular (e.g. for PCN, G-tube, biliary drain).
  • In the Diagnosis/Indication section, clearly state the actual reason for performing the procedure rather than simply copying the HPI.
  • Example: “History of head and neck cancer with unsafe oral intake requiring long-term enteral feeding,” rather than simply “head and neck cancer” for G-tube placement.
  • My personal dictation templates are frequently updated. If you are using my templates, please ensure you are using the most current version rather than older saved copies.
  • All line procedures should specify line size and number of lumens (e.g., 5 F single-lumen; 14.5 F dual-lumen, 17-cm tip-to-cuff).
  • There is no need to document doses of lidocaine, antibiotics, glucagon, nitroglycerin, heparin, etc. as these are recorded elsewhere in the chart.
  • Sedation medication doses must always be documented for billing purposes.
  • Also include dosing for very "potent" meds, including tPA, absolute alcohol, and thrombin.
